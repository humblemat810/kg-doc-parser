The proposed replacement for text-only duplicate detection is adopted here as
the governing semantic rule: ownership is determined by canonical source
occurrence and character position, not by normalized title or excerpt text.
Repeated text is valid when it occurs at distinct source coordinates; the same
coordinate cannot be owned twice. This rule applies to standalone PageIndex,
all workflow strategies, and all provider modes that share the semantic path.

### Canonical terminal-ownership invariant

For a source `S`, let `M(S)` be its non-whitespace character positions. Let
`Owners(T)` be terminal content-owning spans in the parsed tree. PAGE,
DOCUMENT_ROOT, inherited aggregate extents, and ancestor pointers are not
terminal owners. A heading may be an internal container and must retain its
title through an explicit heading-text leaf when the format represents it.

Every successful parse must satisfy:

- every position in `M(S)` is owned by exactly one terminal content span;
- whitespace-only gaps are allowed, but non-whitespace gaps and multiply-owned
  positions are errors;
- every child span is contained in its parent and its authoritative source
  cluster;
- a successful parent refinement conserves the verified ownership of that
  parent after repair, dedupe, and every other child mutation;
- a zero-child provider response is explicitly recorded as `atomic_retained`
  or another truthful no-op state, never accepted as a vacuous partition;
- the final audit reports terminal coverage separately from any legacy union or
  wrapper coverage metric.
